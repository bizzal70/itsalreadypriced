*Issue #010 · Week of September 06, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The week's honest exploits were boring by design: no reentrancy poetry, just funds that quietly walked out a side door while an audit was the only thing paying attention. Meanwhile the crowd chased tokenized AMC shares onto the "Robinhood chain" and argued about which flavor of synthetic equity is the least bad. When your biggest custody failure is discovered by an auditor rather than a block explorer, the call is coming from inside the house.

## The Breaches

The clean number this week is **$7 million**. **Orionx**, the **Tether**-backed Latin American exchange, is shutting down permanently after an audit found that more than **$7 million** in customer assets had moved to wallets outside its custody. Note the mechanics: this is not a clever contract drain, it is assets leaving controlled addresses, which is the oldest failure mode in the book and the hardest to spin. The exchange is closing rather than clawing back, which tells you what the recovery math looked like.

The larger financial number belongs to the **Trump** and **Melania** meme coins, resurfacing this week in the accounting: roughly **50 wallets** in profit against about **2 million** wallets in loss. Not a hack, just the designed outcome of a launch where the house holds the supply. It priced in the moment it launched; the rest was distribution.

On the recovery side, **Ukrainian police** dismantled a crypto scam operation that was pulling in as much as **$1 million a month**, and a **Massachusetts** man, **Luciano Schipelliti**, was sentenced for the **Superstar Fund** and **TTM Fund** investment scams. Both were frauds, both are now case numbers.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Trezor's ShipMonk breach widens.** Another **67,000** US customers had names, emails, phone numbers, shipping addresses, and order numbers exposed via shipping provider **ShipMonk**, with records dating from **2019 to 2021**. The hardware is fine; the metadata is not. Some records predate the **90-day** retention window that partners supposedly agreed to, which is its own quiet failure. A hardware-wallet customer list is a phishing target list, full stop.
- **ClickFix payloads stored on-chain.** Over **5,400 hacked sites** are serving **ClickFix** payloads hosted in smart contracts on **BNB Smart Chain**. Putting the malware delivery layer on an immutable ledger is takedown-resistant by design; the chain is now the C2.
- **Invisible Unicode phishing.** **Microsoft** flagged a high-volume campaign splitting lure words like "funding" with invisible Unicode tag characters to slip past email filters. Cheap, effective, and aimed squarely at anyone whose inbox is their weakest key.
- **REVSTEALER's persistence modules.** Four programs (**ProManager**, **WinUpdate**, **SoftManager**, and one more) linger after the stealer self-deletes, with one disabling **Windows Update** and **Defender** before running a crypto miner. See below.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The scam-compound economy got official numbers this week. **FinCEN** tied roughly **$12.7 billion** to crypto scams run largely from **Southeast Asian** compounds by transnational criminal organizations, with monthly reported sums up **18%** on average and the model now spreading beyond the region. The **Lights in Dark Rooms** documentary is a useful reminder that many of the people running pig-butchering scripts are themselves trafficking victims held in those compounds. The **Bulkoil**/**TVF** "unlisted in public markets" pitch circulating this week is the retail-facing end of exactly this machine.

On the malware side, **REVSTEALER** and its miner-dropping modules are the familiar quiet drain: infect, disable defenses, mine, persist. No headline theft, just borrowed compute. Elsewhere, attackers are hijacking **MikroTik** routers over internet-exposed **SSH** with no authentication, and exploiting an unpatched **Magento/Adobe Commerce** zero-day (**StyleSmuggler**) to backdoor stores. None of it is crypto-native, all of it feeds the same credential-and-wallet supply chain.

## The Bigger Picture

The board moved on rails, not on hacks. **August payrolls** printed **162,000** jobs, roughly five times the prior year's average, and **Bitcoin** shed about **$1,430** in five minutes as **Polymarket** traders flipped a September rate hike to the majority outcome. **BTC** slid back below **$80,000** after briefly clearing **$81,000**. Worth noting: **CoinDesk** checked six years of data and found NFP is not historically a big **Bitcoin** mover, so treat the reflex sell as positioning, not prophecy. US spot **Bitcoin** ETFs still drew **$731 million** in a single day, the largest since January.

The structural story is tokenized equity. **AMC's** CEO **Adam Aron** called **Robinhood's** stock tokens "contemptible" and "vile" and threatened securities counsel; **Robinhood's** **Dan Gallagher** replied "Send your lawyers and we'll educate them." Meanwhile a meme coin called **BONER** cornered half of **Hims & Hers** tokenized shares, which tells you how seriously the market treats the "equity" part. **The Defiant** documented that **Robinhood Chain's** transaction data went missing from **Ethereum** for **14 minutes** while blocks kept coming every **101 milliseconds** — availability without settlement, which is the whole risk in one sentence.

On the plumbing: **OpenReserve** (a16z-backed) and **Revolut** secured preliminary **OCC** approval for national bank charters with crypto and stablecoin plans, and a **Bank of Korea** study warned dollar-backed stablecoins can push local currencies lower. The banks-are-the-bottleneck thesis is becoming banks-are-the-product. And the **G7** is now urging post-quantum migration while **QuFi** ships a verification platform on **Bitcoin** testnet, a reminder that **Satoshi's** dormant coins remain the single most valuable unpatched attack surface in the space. This week, **600 BTC** of Satoshi-era rewards moved after 16 years, with no confirmed link to Satoshi. Already priced, until it very suddenly is not.
