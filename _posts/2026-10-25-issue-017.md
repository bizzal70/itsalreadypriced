*Issue #017 · Week of October 25, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The week's connective tissue is not a drainer crew or a bridge hack, it is the quiet realization that the tooling meant to automate trust is where trust goes to die. **x402** will happily let an AI agent sign away its full token balance, AI coding agents will install whatever an **AGENTS.md** file in your Downloads tells them to, and **Bitcoin Core** has rewritten its disclosure policy because a one-line prompt now finds bugs that used to take a researcher a month. The exploits, meanwhile, were the usual: governance takeovers and unauthorized mints, draining real money while everyone watched the mempool in real time.

## The Breaches

The headline drain belongs to **X100**, where a governance takeover on **Ethereum** turned treasury tokens and liquidity positions into roughly **4.85 million USDT** across two transactions, with **Blockaid** flagging the attack as ongoing. Proceeds were promptly laundered through the standard playbook: the first exploiter swapped about **4.75 million USDT** into ETH, and funds were fanned out into four separate 400-ETH wallets, with a third transaction pulling **549,916 X** and some USDT from a **Uniswap** pool that was left holding under 200 USDT.

**Initia** halted its entire L1 and rollups after an attacker minted tokens and moved assets to **Ethereum**, with analyst **Spreek** estimating about **$2.6 million** cashed out. The blast radius caught **Agora**, which froze an **AUSD** account after roughly **1.4 million AUSD** left a bridge adapter and was swapped for ETH, while insisting it has no contracts deployed on Initia.

Elsewhere, **Cysic**'s bridge was exploited to mint 10 million tokens on **BNB Smart Chain**; the attacker routed **$744,000** in ETH and BNB into **Tornado Cash** and ignored the dangling 10% bounty. **CoinDCX** quietly acknowledged wallet-sweeping losses after first denying a new breach, with **Tether** blacklisting USDT wallets holding about **$2.2 million** per **AMLBot**. And **India's** largest exchange denied a hack after sleuths traced **$16 million** in suspicious outflows, which is the kind of denial that ages in public.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **The x402 machine-payment flaw** is the one to internalize. Per **V12**, AI agents paying for API calls through the **x402 SDKs** could sign a payment for their entire balance of a supported token if a malicious API simply asked, because the default client set no spending limit until August 18. Autonomous signing plus no cap equals a blank check.
- **AGENTS.md as an attack vector.** Researchers found a malicious **AGENTS.md** instruction file sitting in a Downloads folder that tricked an AI coding agent into installing a rogue npm package. The agent did exactly what it was told, which is the problem.
- **Electrum** patched a flaw where a malicious server substitutes a fake payment and tricks the user into signing it while raising the fee; developers confirmed at least one user lost money.
- **Succinct** patched an **SP1** bug (native Rust verification, versions 6.0.0 through 6.8.0) that could let invalid proofs pass. Onchain Groth16 and Plonk verification were unaffected, which is the only reason this is a footnote and not a headline.
- **Swell** paused swETH and rswETH deposits and withdrawals to correct NAV, blocking withdrawal requests and ETH claims onchain while it sorts the accounting.
- **Ethereum** delayed the **Glamsterdam** Hoodi testnet fork after **ChainSecurity** found gas changes that could lock contracts into failure states, citing 13 real-world cases. Better caught on Hoodi than mainnet.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

No single marquee crew this week, but the drainer and phishing machinery kept grinding. A **Trust Wallet** impersonation email campaign is pushing fake "update" downloads to a malicious site, the perennial reminder that wallet updates come through app stores, not inboxes. **Socket** found 16 fake **Rabby** and **OKX Wallet** extensions on **Firefox**, unpublished by **Mozilla** around Oct. 5, though anyone whose recovery phrase was already slurped is not saved by a takedown.

On the hardware side, **Keystone** suffered a website breach exposing customer addresses and order details; the company says payment info, recovery phrases, and private keys were not exposed, while customers point out the data was supposedly deleted 180 days after orders. Physical-address leaks are a wrench-attack shopping list, delete-policy or not. In the broader threat landscape, researchers documented ransomware affiliates of **INC** and **The Gentlemen** running an AI-driven intrusion framework dubbed **Phantom**, and **Interpol**'s Operation Serengeti III recovered **$105 million** and made 1,462 arrests across 21 countries, shutting illegal crypto mines along the way.

## The Bigger Picture

The institutional plumbing kept advancing regardless of price. **HSBC** switched on its Hong Kong dollar stablecoin inside **PayMe**, dropping a bank-issued token into over 3 million wallets, while **Singapore**, **Malaysia**, and **Thailand** expanded the **BLOOM** cross-border stablecoin initiative with **Ripple**, **Visa**, and **Mastercard** in the mix. **Franklin Templeton** added **$150 million** in fund shares to the **XRP Ledger**, all in one wallet.

Regulators are redrawing the map. The **SEC** named tokenization and AI "super intelligence" as systemic risks in its 2027 examination priorities, while cryptocurrencies as a standalone category remain conspicuously absent. **South Korea** moved to regulate stablecoins as payment instruments under its forthcoming Digital Asset Basic Act, and **China** set a 2030 target for a national blockchain network while keeping its crypto restrictions intact. In a sign of the times, **a16z** dropped "crypto" from its fund name, rebranding to **a16z Frontier** to chase AI and robotics, which tells you where the narrative capital is flowing.

Markets, as context: spot **Bitcoin ETFs** bled **$635.6 million** on Thursday before clawing back to about $301 million in net inflows for the week, as BTC briefly slipped below **$80,000** and crypto longs lost **$404 million** in four hours. **Ether** fell under **$2,300**, down 14.7% over 28 days. By the time it is news, it is already priced in, and this week the market had long since decided to wait and see.
