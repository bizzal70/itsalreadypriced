*Issue #014 · Week of October 04, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

North Korea's 2026 ledger crossed ten figures on the back of a single exchange breach, which tells you everything about where the real money is lost. Meanwhile two separate exploiters returned funds under pressure, one quadrillion phantom dollars got minted into existence, and a $2.3 billion L2 shut down for the price of a sandwich. The attackers are getting richer, the defenders are getting faster, and the vanity metrics are getting louder.

## The Breaches

The headline number belongs to **Bitget**. **Chainalysis** traced the September 24 breach, roughly **$387 million**, back to **North Korea**, pushing the DPRK's 2026 haul past **$1 billion**. The firm detailed chasing stolen funds across four blockchains, with onchain records showing stolen **XRP** swapped through **THORChain**. Bitget has since rebuilt its protection fund to **$309 million** and restored withdrawals on major assets. The money is gone; the optics are managed.

The more interesting story is money that came back. **NEAR Intents** lost roughly **$3.8 million** in a Thursday exploit, then its GM identified the attacker, posted three return addresses, and issued a 48-hour ultimatum. The attacker complied, returning about **34.59 BTC** in full. Call it a bullish hack if you must; it is really just a reminder that pseudonymity is not anonymity.

Smaller but instructive: an attacker exploited a **third-party adapter** to drain about **$305,000** from two **Safe** multisig wallets. **Aave** founder **Stani Kulechov** was quick to note that **Aave v3** itself was unaffected, which is true and also the entire point. The perimeter is never the contract you audited; it is the integration you did not.

Then there is **Zano**, where an exploiter minted more than a quadrillion **fUSD** (and some **369 million** unauthorized ZANO) that were cryptographically indistinguishable from the real thing. The team's only remedy was a blockchain rollback, the nuclear option that every maximalist swears is impossible until it is their chain.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Arbitrum paused new Stylus activations** in an emergency security action. Active contracts can still run and renew, and a new **BoLD** guard can delay unconfirmed withdrawals if contradictory proofs are accepted. Pausing a feature before it bites you is the rarest form of discipline in this industry.
- **Core Lightning** warned that attackers are actively targeting unpatched nodes. Anyone running version **26.06.7** or earlier was told to upgrade immediately. Lightning's attack surface grows every time someone forgets it is real software.
- **Drift** opened exploit recovery claims with initial payouts of just over **1%** of user losses, no firm timeline for the rest. The gap between "funds recovered" and "funds returned" is where hope goes to die.
- **Beyond crypto, patch anyway:** a critical **FortiMail** zero-day (**CVE-2026-104286**, CVSS 9.8) is under active exploitation and in CISA's KEV catalog, two maximum-severity **Dell CSM** flaws (one CVSS 10.0) give unauthenticated admin and root on Kubernetes nodes, and **GitLab** patched a 9.9 AI Gateway RCE on self-hosted instances. The infrastructure your exchange runs on does not care that you are decentralized.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

**North Korea** remains the market mover, full stop. The **Bitget** attribution is what pushed its 2026 total past a billion, and the laundering pattern (cross-chain hops, THORChain swaps) is the same playbook, just at scale.

Elsewhere, the social layer stayed the weakest link. **Microsoft's** official **X** account, 13 million followers, was hijacked to shill a pump-and-dump token. If Redmond can get its account taken over, your favorite protocol's intern can too. On the enforcement side, a suspected **ShinyHunters** member known as "Rey" (**Saif al-Din Khader**) was reportedly detained in Jordan on September 29 and is cooperating with the **FBI** to identify other members. Spanish police arrested a 16-year-old accused of running the **KillSec** ransomware group, with a second suspect facing extradition. And the **US Treasury** designated Russia's **A7 Network** a transnational criminal organization, targeting the front companies behind a ruble token that moved **$179 billion**. **Japan** separately added **Garantex** to its Russia sanctions list, catching up to everyone else.

Offline, the wrench attacks continue: armed men forced a victim to open cold wallets during a home invasion. Self-custody has no password reset, and the attackers know it.

## The Bigger Picture

The board shifted most in Washington and Brussels. The **Clarity Act** stalled in the Senate (ethics disputes over the president's crypto interests did not help, and a third **TRUMP** meme coin dinner for the top 185 holders is not helping now). **BitGo** CEO **Mike Belshe** warned that Clarity's failure leaves capital markets exposed to a single firm holding exchange, brokerage, and custody functions, a concentration risk he compared to Lehman. Meanwhile **Trump** is expected to name **Jay Clayton**, the SEC chair who sued **Ripple**, as AI czar. The regulatory memory is long.

On the structural side, the **ICBA** sued the **OCC** over crypto trust charters, arguing they are a "side door" into banking without the safeguards. In Europe, **Circle** pushed back on **MiCA**'s bank-deposit reserve mandates, and the **ECB** outlined three models for putting central bank money onchain. Tokenization kept grinding forward regardless: **BNB Chain** crossed **$1 billion** in tokenized stocks and ETFs, and **Centrifuge** put three tokenized funds live on **Arc**.

As for markets, **Bitcoin** tapped roughly **$87,000** after a weak US jobs report (just **29,000** added, unemployment at **4.2%**) erased October rate-hike odds, and spot ETFs logged **$2.65 billion** in September inflows. The macro did the work, as it always does.

And the quiet obituary: **Blast**, once a **$2.3 billion** L2, is winding down after earning **$110** in chain revenue in a single day. TVL is a loan; revenue is the rent. Blast forgot to charge rent.
