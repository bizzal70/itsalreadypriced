*Issue #015 · Week of October 11, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The threat model everyone ignored turned out to be the cheapest one: a reseller shipping pre-compromised hardware. While the industry argued about quantum apocalypse timelines, **CryptoBilis** was moving eight figures out of **Ledger** devices that looked factory-fresh. Self-custody is only as trustworthy as the supply chain it rode in on.

## The Breaches

The headline number is **Ledger**, or more precisely the reseller **CryptoBilis**. Ledger confirmed an unauthorized hardware implant in at least one affected device, with on-chain investigators tracking suspected losses from **$72 million** to roughly **$90 million** depending on who is counting. The attack was isolated to a Southeast Asian reseller, which is cold comfort if you bought there. Buyers from the trailing 90 days were told to delay setup or migrate to a new device with a fresh seed, and CryptoBilis halted all hardware wallet inventory, not just Ledger's.

The laundering was faster than the disclosure. A wallet linked to the thefts routed **464 ETH** to **Tornado Cash** through an intermediary and four deposit wallets, keeping about 700 ETH in reserve. On Tron, stolen funds converted **2 million USDT** into **USDD** through its stability module, stepping neatly outside **Tether's** freeze controls. **PeckShield** traced **$3.89 million** of the proceeds to **Binance** deposits, giving investigators an exchange lead that intermediary wallets promptly muddied. Tether froze an estimated **$10 million** across the broader cluster. It is a tidy demonstration that freeze controls work right up until money reaches a stablecoin whose issuer does not cooperate.

Elsewhere, **Liquid** Bitcoin redemptions remain frozen at **85.8%** reserve coverage, a roughly **603 BTC** gap, with peg-outs paused pending full backing and a completed security review.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **XRP Ledger's decade-old mint bug.** The **XRPL** disclosed a flaw, fixed in **xrpld 3.4.1** on Sept. 25, that could have created trillions of XRP from nothing. Developers found no evidence of exploitation on a public network. Ten years is a long time for a loaded gun to sit unfired.
- **EIP-7702 as an exfiltration path.** **$3.55 million** in unclaimed bridge funds moved via **EIP-7702** account authorizations. **BlockSec** notes the authorizations were valid, which does not establish whether the owner knowingly approved anything. The new account-abstraction surface is already being probed.
- **Coldcard's X account phishing.** A phishing link appeared on **Coldcard's** official X account pointing to a fake migration site built to harvest recovery phrases. Coldcard found no matching login record, which raises more questions than it answers. Even the paranoid hardware crowd is a social-engineering target.
- **AnyDesk Linux pre-auth root.** A working exploit is now public for a pre-authentication RCE in **AnyDesk Linux**, patched quietly in 8.0.3 back in June with no CVE and a changelog that called root access "a crash." Silent patches age badly.
- **AhsayCBS miners.** Two unpatched flaws (**CVE-2026-105133** among them) in the **AhsayCBS** backup platform are being exploited to drop webshells and **XMRig** miners disguised as Microsoft Edge.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

No Lazarus sighting this week, but plenty of competition. The **CryptoBilis** supply-chain operation is the standout, combining physical tampering with a rehearsed laundering pipeline through Tornado Cash, USDD, and exchange deposits.

On the AI-abuse front, a Chinese actor used the **ARTEX AI** penetration suite and **Claude** agents against South Korean banks, while separate campaigns abused **Google Ads** and **Bing** redirects to push fake **Claude** installers delivering **ClickFix** payloads. **Flax Typhoon**, another China-linked group, drove five flaws into **CISA's** KEV catalog, including a 10.0-scored **ProFTPD** bug from 2015. A credential-stealing **GitHub Actions** campaign compromised maintainer accounts (including the author of the 18,400-star pyxel engine) to plant malicious workflows across 340-plus repositories. The **P7 DarkSword** iOS exploit kit added on-device keychain and crypto-wallet theft.

On the enforcement side, the **FBI** arrested another suspected **ShinyHunters** member tied to its jobs-portal breach, Germany arrested an alleged core **Qilin** ransomware member, and a dual citizen pleaded guilty to running a 15,000-strong money-mule network.

## The Bigger Picture

Quantum anxiety is now a product roadmap. **Zcash** targets hash-based signatures for January after a "bunker mode" scare, **Tron** claims post-quantum cryptography live on testnet, and **Starknet** floated becoming an L1 to pursue full quantum resistance by 2027 (the token jumped 20-30% on the maybe). **Dragonfly's** Qureshi offered the sober counterpoint: asset migration is not a real fix if models break signatures, so build proactive defenses now.

Markets stayed soft. US spot Bitcoin ETFs shed **$679 million** on the week, a **$920 million** swing, with Ether ETFs extending a nine-day outflow streak and Bitcoin consolidating near **$82,500** on the one-year anniversary of the 10/10 flash crash. Nobody learned much; altcoin liquidity still looks fragile.

Regulation did the heavy lifting. The **CLARITY Act** died in the Senate, leaving the **SEC** and **CFTC** to set market structure by rule rather than statute (a win for incumbents who can afford lawyers). The CFTC moved to fold event contracts into the swap definition while carving out casino-style wagers, teeing up a jurisdictional fight as **Blockchain.com** files for DCM and FCM licenses ahead of a $500 million IPO. The **UK** sanctioned crypto exchanges tied to Russia's A7 network, the US telegraphed a **$1 billion** Iran-linked seizure, and New York secured up to **$35 million** and a lifetime ban from Celsius founder **Alex Mashinsky**. The grift gets litigated eventually; it just takes years, and by then it is already priced.
