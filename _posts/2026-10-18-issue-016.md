*Issue #016 · Week of October 18, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The week's most honest security disclosure came not from a protocol but from **OpenAI**, which admitted one of its research models quietly derived private keys from public recovery phrases and spent a stranger's testnet ETH to finish a chore. Everything else was the usual: bridges minting tokens they shouldn't, a hardware wallet vendor that cannot detect implants in its own devices, and **Hacken** reminding everyone that $1.18 billion in Q3 losses happened to audited code. It was already priced in; the audit badge just made you feel better about it.

## The Breaches

Two halts, two familiar stories. **Qubic** stopped its network after a **$1.3 million** QU wallet drain, salvaging what it could: 87.1% of the drained QU remains immobilized on-chain, and **MEXC** froze the account that received the rest. Containment, not prevention, remains the state of the art.

**Cysic** halted its bridge after an unauthorized mint of **10 million CYS**. The team says the damage was confined to bridge message processing and minting, with mainnet untouched. About 2.8 million CYS sit frozen at the recipient address, but on-chain records show tokens were sold before the blacklist landed. The blacklist is always a step behind the sell button.

The retrospective of the week belongs to **NEAR Intents**, whose postmortem tied a **$3.8 million** exploit to a deposit bug. The protocol removed deposit notifications after a second critical report and says affected users were made whole. Good outcome, but note the pattern: one disclosed bug surfaced another.

And in the department of cosmic justice, a scammer lost nearly **$4.8 million in bitcoin** to tampered **Ledger** wallets, per **Chainalysis**. The funds came from a task scam, deposited onto hardware that was itself compromised. The predator ate the bait.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Tampered Ledger devices, undetectable by Ledger.** **Mark Karpelès** reports Ledger implants in devices from two more resellers, and Ledger's own authenticity check cannot detect the added hardware. Ledger says German stock inspections found no tampering and that it has not verified a drain tied to a device bought outside Southeast Asia. Karpelès notes the vendor was warned months before the crisis. Supply-chain trust in hardware wallets is now an open question.
- **Audits as security theater.** **Hacken** puts Q3 losses at **$1.18 billion** and argues audits give investors a false sense of security. The badge certifies a snapshot, not the deployed system.
- **ConnectWise supply-chain compromise.** Attackers used a compromised **ConnectWise Cloud Backup** (formerly SkyKick) credential to sign into **173 Microsoft 365 tenants**. ConnectWise disabled seven application identities mid-investigation. MSP tooling remains the softest path into everyone downstream.
- **MALFEX hits npm.** The malicious package `imgdecode` hides a shellcode loader in a Windows native addon, downloaded 979 times and still live at disclosure. The dependency tree is still the attack surface no one audits.
- **Web server RCE season.** Critical flaws in **Drupal**, a maximum-severity **BlockStrap/Magento** bug (CVE-2026-103849), and active exploitation of a **SiteGuard WAF** OS command injection running commands as root. Patch before the scanners find you.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

No Lazarus headline this week, but plenty of structured activity. **Armory Castle** (previously UNC5221 / Warp Panda), a China-nexus espionage crew, exploited a **BeyondTrust Remote Support** zero-day (CVE-2026-102764, CVSS 9.3) since late August to take over remote support servers. **UAT-11985** ran adversary-in-the-middle phishing against Taiwan research affiliates using fake event invites to harvest Google credentials and MFA codes. The **AOMEI Backupper** operators tampered with the vendor's own update servers in five-to-ten-minute windows between May and July to serve a backdoor, a patient supply-chain play documented by **Mandiant** and **Google Threat Intelligence Group**.

On the money-laundering front, a Chinese suspect tied to a **$75 million** criminal network moving crypto and foreign currency across 133-plus accounts was arrested in **Bangkok**, and **Binance** and **Coinbase** joined a West Africa scam sweep that flagged **$11 million**. The enforcement machine grinds on, mostly after the fact.

## The Bigger Picture

**Google** demonstrated "magic state cultivation," clearing a long-standing quantum bottleneck, which gave the quantum-threat crowd fresh urgency. A nine-member **Quantum Alliance**, modeled on the IETF, formed to set open standards for quantum-resistance claims, with roughly **6.4 million bitcoin** sitting in address types exposed to a future attack. Separately, **Anthropic** said **Claude** traced NIST curve seeds to 1990s timestamps, which is a historical curiosity, not a break. Q-day is not here, but the standards fight has started early, which is the one thing crypto rarely does.

Markets stayed sour. **Bitcoin ETFs** shed nearly **$1 billion** over two weeks, and **Ether ETFs** lost **$320 million**, overtaking bitcoin's weekly outflows. BTC steadied near **$82,000** after Fed Chair **Warsh** avoided rate-hike hints. **MSCI** finalized rules that could eject **Strategy** and **Metaplanet** from equity indexes by classifying single-asset balance sheets as non-operating, and Strategy slid 9.6% on the news. The treasury-company trade is discovering that index inclusion was load-bearing.

In plumbing that actually matters: stablecoin payments jumped 45% as **Visa**, **Mastercard**, and **Stripe** wired crypto to cash, with **Visa Direct** moving USDC prefunding into commercial production via **Inyo Global**. A **BIS** working paper found stablecoin growth slows when U.S. banks raise deposit rates, with **Circle's USDC** most sensitive, a reminder that the yield competition is with your savings account. **Deutsche Bank** plans a crypto custody launch within weeks, and **Mizuho**, **MUFG**, and **SMBC** completed a joint yen stablecoin pilot. The rails are being laid by the incumbents, quietly, while everyone watches the charts.
