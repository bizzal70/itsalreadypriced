*Issue #011 · Week of September 13, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The novelty this week was watching two very different institutions get robbed with a straight face: **Blockstream** negotiating a **Liquid** ransom in the open on-chain, and **Revolut** couriering customers' passports to a fraudster who simply asked nicely from a government domain. Meanwhile **Anthropic** published the confession the industry keeps pretending is hypothetical: its own model is already doing the work. None of this is surprising. It was all priced in the moment we decided convenience and automation were security features.

## The Breaches

The headline number is **Blockstream's Liquid** exploit. As of Sept. 8, roughly **3,400 BTC had been returned and about 598.5 BTC (~$47M) remained outstanding**, with transactions resumed but peg-outs disabled during recovery. What makes this one worth reading the chain for is the venue: the "white hat" framing collapsed into an extortion negotiation conducted publicly via **OP_RETURN**. Blockstream's answer was blunt: it is theft, no ransom, and they will bring in law enforcement, exchanges, and forensics. Correct posture. Whether it recovers the last 600 BTC is a separate question from whether paying would have set the going rate for every federation operator watching.

The other real-money event was data, not coin. **Revolut** fulfilled a fraudulent information request sent from a genuine **government agency email domain**, exposing passports, selfies, and full **Bitcoin transaction histories** for a "limited" number of users. **ZachXBT** speculated the targeting was aimed at high-net-worth users, which is the only rational read: a KYC dossier tying an identity to on-chain activity is a wrench-attack shopping list. The fintech control that failed here was not cryptographic. It was a human trusting a From: header.

For scale, one circulating report tallies **$3.63 billion stolen since 2025**, with CEXes bleeding through private-key compromise and DEXes through contract exploits and fake UIs. The mix has not changed. The perpetrators have professionalized.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **GitLab CVE-2026-85706 (CVSS 10.0):** An unauthenticated path traversal in the repository commits API allowing arbitrary file reads. In-the-wild probing began within hours of disclosure. If you self-host, you were already late when you read this.
- **JFrog Artifactory chain:** Attackers chained two flaws to bypass auth, gain admin, and plant a **Rust backdoor** on self-hosted servers ( **Wiz** observed activity Aug. 15 to Sept. 8). Patches predated the attacks, so this is a patch-hygiene failure, not a zero-day. Now on **CISA's KEV**.
- **Cisco Secure FMC CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0):** Auth bypass exploited by three clusters, including **Qilin** ransomware operators. Firewall management planes are the keys to the kingdom; treat them accordingly.
- **Check Point VPN (CVE-2026-85102/85103):** The Dutch **NCSC** warns exploitation is imminent. Also newly on KEV: **ScreenConnect** and **MikroTik RouterOS** flaws.
- **Trezor / Brevo:** A login flaw at email vendor **Brevo** exposed **347,000 Trezor subscriber addresses**; 2,500 users clicked the phishing link. Trezor is treating every address as compromised. Your seed was safe; your inbox was the attack surface.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The week belonged to **Anthropic's** disclosure that threat actors are actively using **Claude** across the kill chain. It branded them **Generative Threat Groups**: a Russian state-sponsored cluster (**GTG-20006**, aligned with **Midnight Blizzard**) used Claude to rebuild malware after detection; a Russian-speaking operator hit **20-plus organizations**; a Mali-based consultant built a mass-surveillance platform. Separately, hackers abused Claude to extract secrets from **1.8 million Android apps**, and **OpenAI agents** were tied to the May **RubyGems** supply-chain attack that reached RCE on **RubyDoc** servers. Anthropic also disrupted **industrial-scale distillation** from seven China-based labs. The through-line: automation cuts the cost of competent attacks, which means more of them, not scarier ones.

Elsewhere, **Microsoft** detailed passkey- and SSO-themed social engineering by **ShinyHunters**, **Helix**, and other extortion crews to breach **Microsoft 365** and exfiltrate data, plus a million-message CEO-fraud blast. And **North Korea** continues staffing the pipeline the old-fashioned way, using third-country IT workers to pass interviews before **DPRK** operatives take the seat. The passkey angle deserves a note: the phish targets the enrollment and fallback flow, not the credential. Phishing-resistant does not mean social-engineering-resistant.

## The Bigger Picture

Macro did the heavy lifting. Hotter-than-forecast **core CPI (+0.3% in August)** pushed **Fed** hike odds toward the mid-60s, the 30-year Treasury yield cleared five years of closes, and **Brent** topped $106. Bitcoin sank below **$77,000** before a brief rebound toward **$79K**; **CryptoQuant** insists the tape needs to clear **$81,700** (and up to $88,700) to confirm a new leg. **Bitcoin ETFs** shed **$449M over three days**. Two golden crosses flickered and failed. This is what a market without a bid looks like.

On the structural side, the **CLARITY Act** returned as Schrödinger's legislation ahead of a cloture vote, with revised text adding **CFTC registration** and **Bank Secrecy Act** obligations for "non-decentralized" trading protocols and confining the DeFi title to spot commodity transactions. **Lummis** claims 100-plus Democrat-requested changes. Read it as the on-ramp for supervised finance, because that is what it is.

The adoption stories were quieter and more durable: **India's SEBI Demat 2.0** pilot issued **$107M in tokenized corporate bonds** settled via wholesale CBDC, the opening move on a $620B market. **Circle** paid **$400M for Tazapay's** emerging-market rails. **Ripple** is pitching **RLUSD** at corporate treasuries and stuffing AI agents into its own $1B treasury bet. And **Bitwise** is euthanizing its **Dogecoin ETF** after ten months and roughly $688K in assets, a tidy reminder that a wrapper does not manufacture demand. Also filed under "priced in": **TRM** found that of **$52.7M** across 198.9M **x402** settlements, almost none is actually AI agents spending money. The autonomous-commerce narrative is early. The autonomous-attack narrative, per Anthropic, is not.
