*Issue #009 · Week of August 30, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The theme this week is the gap between "patched" and "actually patched." **Cosmos Labs** shipped a fix it believed closed the door, then watched six chains get drained through it, while **Rain** learned that a contract you forgot to update is a contract someone else will read for you. Bitcoin flirted with $81K, **Warsh** said the quiet part loud, and the ancient wallets started moving. None of it changes the arithmetic: shared code is shared risk, and the bill always arrives.

## The Breaches

The headline event is the **Cosmos EVM** balance-handling flaw (**GHSA-7g4w-cg88-2cq2**), exploited between August 20 and 25 to drain roughly **$5.7 million** across six blockchains. **MANTRA Chain** absorbed the largest single loss at **$3.6 million** and disputes the timeline: it says the patch landed only 20 hours before the attack began and did not name the flaw it was quietly fixing. The bug shipped without a CVE, without a CWE classification, and without a CVSS score, which is a fitting epitaph for a vulnerability that maintainers believed they had already cleared. When you share a module across an ecosystem, you share the exploit too, and everyone patches on the slowest chain's schedule.

Second on the ledger is the **Rain** card contract exploit on **Solana**, which **Avici** reports cost **1,685 users $500,859**. The attacker used an outdated version of Rain's contract that a small number of programs were still running. A related **CoinDesk** account puts the figure near **$1.1 million** and notes the issuing neobank's token dropped **49%** on the news. Both **Rain** and **Avici** say affected users will be made whole. Credit where due: the disclosure was clean and prompt, which the community noticed favorably against the muddier **Metabase** handling weeks prior.

Rounding out the drains, **The Sandbox** suffered a bridge exploit and pledged **1:1 SAND repayment** from treasury using pre-incident balances on **Base** and **BNB Chain**, without inflating its fixed 3 billion supply cap. And **Fogo**, a Solana-adjacent Layer 1, halted mainnet after an attacker received **400 million FOGO** (about 10% of circulating supply, worth roughly **$3 million** at the time). A halted chain is the modern circuit breaker: it works, and it tells you exactly how decentralized you weren't.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Polygon disclosed flaws fixed in recent hard forks** carrying denial-of-service and validator resource risks. Patched before public disclosure, which is how it is supposed to go, and a quiet contrast to the Cosmos sequence.
- **Three CVSS 10.0 ServiceNow AI Platform flaws** allow unauthenticated code injection, SQL injection, and privilege escalation. Hosted instances were patched; self-hosted and partner-managed instances are the exposure. Enterprise tooling remains the soft underbelly of every treasury operation.
- **GiveWP** for WordPress carries a maximum-severity unauthenticated RCE, part of a broader batch of five critical **WordPress** plugin and theme flaws (**Avada**, **TranslatePress**, **Pods**, **WPMU DEV Dashboard**). Donation portals process crypto too.
- **Over 8,300 Gitea instances** remain unpatched against a critical RCE already under active exploitation. Your CI pipeline is your supply chain.
- **ownCloud CVE-2023-49105** was added to **CISA's KEV** catalog after a Chinese-speaking actor used it to hit a Philippine nuclear research body. A three-year-old bug still paying dividends.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The drainer economy had a busy week in your browser. Researchers flagged **19 Chrome and Edge extensions** (18 Chrome, 1 Edge) carrying wallet-secret theft and crypto-draining code, published over the prior six months and linked by shared tradecraft. A separate cluster delivered a modular malware framework deploying **ClickFix** lures alongside data theft. On the social-engineering front, **Microsoft** disclosed **TerminalFix**, a ClickFix variant that steers victims into **Windows Terminal** or **PowerShell** rather than the Run dialog, raising the odds that a complex command executes cleanly.

Session-token theft continues its quiet expansion beyond wallets: **Anthropic** warned that infostealers on user machines are lifting active **Claude** sessions to consume usage. It is the same primitive that drains exchange accounts, aimed at a new target. Meanwhile **ShinyHunters** claimed **284 million patient records** from **McKesson**, **FulcrumSec** claimed 86 GB from **Manchester Airports Group**, and **Berlin** confirmed it will not pay its extortionists. The pattern holds: infostealer, session, extortion, repeat.

Then there is the crypto grift proper. **Real Trump Coins** denies authorizing the **GOLD** token that crashed roughly 99% after team-linked wallets dumped **224.5 million tokens** in a suspected **$330,000** rug, blaming "bad actors" while questions swirl around its X account and concentrated supply. Also worth a skeptical glance: **upvote.trade**, a Robinhood Chain "reward for old MOON holders" scheme routing through tokenized RDDT with a wallet checker and nothing claimable. Wallet checkers are reconnaissance. Treat them accordingly.

## The Bigger Picture

Markets got their Jackson Hole cold shower. **Fed Chair Kevin Warsh** told the room the central bank has "work to do" on inflation and offered zero rate hints, sending Bitcoin from an overnight **$81,479** high back below **$78,000**, and ending a nine-day spot **Bitcoin ETF** inflow streak with **$201.8 million** in outflows. **Ethereum ETFs**, by contrast, pulled **$226 million** in a day. **Michael Saylor** teased **Strategy's** first purchase in two months. And Bitcoin's oldest coins keep waking: **Galaxy Research** notes six ancient wallets moved **$40 million** in ten days, most avoiding exchanges, which is the opposite of a distribution signal, for now.

On the board that actually matters, regulation split cleanly. The **Ninth Circuit** ruled **Kalshi's** sports contracts are gambling Nevada can regulate, directly contradicting the **Third Circuit** and setting up a **SCOTUS** collision. The lesson generalizes: courts weigh what a product does, not what the filing calls it, and that reasoning points straight at every "it's just software" defense onchain. The **Clarity Act** slipped to September; banks are building anyway. **California's** Senate passed a bill banning memecoin issuance by public officials, arriving too late for the **$4.7 billion** that **Public Citizen** says investors are underwater across Trump ventures.
