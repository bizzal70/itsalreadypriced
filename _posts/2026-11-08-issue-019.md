*Issue #019 · Week of November 08, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The supply chain is the attack surface now, and the vendor is the soft underbelly. **Crédit Mutuel Arkéa** lost $133 million through an outside provider, **Gnosis Pay** leaked card and wallet data through a legacy system, and **Chainalysis**, the firm that tracks everyone else, admitted it exposed credentials for every customer it has. When the watchers get watched, it was already priced in.

## The Breaches

The headline number belongs to **Crédit Mutuel Arkéa**, a French cooperative bank with roughly 5.6 million customers, which confirmed $133 million drained not from client accounts but from the bank's own account, routed through an unnamed third-party service provider. **ZachXBT** had been tracking the flow for days before the attribution landed, watching more than $30 million move eth to monero while he narrated it in public. He was explicit that this was not **Lazarus**. The laundering happening live, under surveillance, tells you how little the watchers can actually interdict.

The **Binance** $6.3 billion heist continues to metabolize. **Elliptic** reports the attackers have swapped roughly $124 million of stolen tokens into bitcoin, including about $50 million in XRP, spreading across more than 20,000 wallets on 16 blockchains. The bulk of the ETH and BTC has not moved, and $2.85 billion remains frozen. **Security Alliance** estimates the intruders held access for eight weeks before pulling the trigger, the same multi-stage pattern seen in the $388 million **Bitget** hack. **TRM Labs** called it cyber war. The CFO departing nine days later was, we are assured, planned six months in advance.

On the data side, **Gnosis Pay** disclosed unauthorized access to its legacy consumer-card system on Nov. 3, exposing card numbers, home addresses, and linked crypto wallets. Funds were untouched, the enterprise platform unaffected, but the **Rotki** founder shared a notification confirming his own address, card, and wallet details were in the blast radius. Doxxing crypto holders by home address is its own category of harm.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Keplr's social-login breach** saw a six-account Cosmos Hub batch tokenize 2,265.59 staked ATOM. Keplr paused Google and Apple login, confirmed **Web3Auth** recovery addresses, and muddied the water by noting some apparent drains were protective recovery transfers. Recovery-phrase and hardware accounts were unaffected, which is the entire lesson.
- **Chainalysis** confirmed a breach exposing credentials and live tokens for all customers. The blockchain intelligence firm is now a blockchain intelligence liability.
- **SonicWall** patched an actively exploited SSL-VPN authentication bypass among seven SonicOS flaws. The MFA bypass is the one that matters.
- **SSHole (CVE-2026-123158)**, an OpenSSH flaw on musl systems with PAM disabled, saw exploitation attempts within hours of disclosure and root on a honeypot. Disclosure-to-exploitation windows keep shrinking.
- **MetaMask** clarified its recent incident did not touch wallet infrastructure, a rare piece of good news in a week where everyone else's did.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

**North Korea** remains the headline act behind the **Binance** intrusion, with **TRM Labs** framing state response as the only viable deterrent. The eight-week dwell time flagged by **Security Alliance** is the tell: this is patient, access-first operation, not smash-and-grab. The **Arkéa** crew, pointedly not Lazarus per **ZachXBT**, demonstrated the other end of the spectrum, converting to monero in real time while under public observation and getting away with the bulk anyway.

Elsewhere, fake security tooling on **GitHub** continues to target researchers with backdoors and infostealers, and **CTM360** flagged gift-card giveaway scams impersonating major brands. The insider threat earned a conviction: ex-**FBI** counterintelligence agent Patrick Yaroch pleaded guilty to stealing $2.47 million in crypto from wallets tied to 35 investigations, using credentials the Bureau itself had collected. He asked ChatGPT for advice on his new life in Portugal. The vineyard will have to wait.

## The Bigger Picture

Regulators spent the week swinging a cleaver. **FinCEN**, under Operation Economic Outcast, cut **MEXC** off from the US financial system over an alleged $16 billion in illicit flows tied to Iran, North Korea, and Russia. **OKX** promptly warned MEXC users of deposit freezes, treating the designation like sanctions. **OFAC** added 45 more **Prince Group** targets and sanctioned eight Canadian money firms over Iran links. In a rare retreat, **Treasury** and the **Fed** withdrew the proposed $250 cross-border travel-rule threshold, leaving the $3,000 line intact.

Markets did not cooperate with anyone's narrative. Bitcoin dipped under $75,000 as the AI-stock rout dragged crypto down with it, over $1 billion in bullish bets liquidated, and spot **Bitcoin ETFs** flipped negative for 2026 after a $1.28 billion outflow week, with **BlackRock's IBIT** accounting for 98% of Friday's bleed. Strong US jobs data (165,000 against a 65,000 estimate) revived December rate-hike bets, and crypto missed the tech rebound entirely.

In the quieter corners, **Vitalik Buterin** repositioned **ETH** as a store of value rather than a fee-burning token, reviving the perennial question of what Ethereum is actually trying to be. The **Democrats'** midterm sweep leaves the **CLARITY Act** facing a harder road, and the **Trump** administration's crypto 401(k) rule entered final White House review. By the time your retirement account can hold altcoins, the thesis will already be priced in.
