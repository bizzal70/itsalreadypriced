*Issue #021 · Week of November 22, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The machines are earning their keep on both sides of the fence this week: AI found a stalling bug in **Bitcoin Core** and an espionage crew pointed autonomous agents at a university server to find and exploit a genuine zero-day. Meanwhile bridges did what bridges do, which is mint tokens nobody deposited. If you were waiting for the quantum apocalypse, the mundane key compromise got there first.

## The Breaches

A week of bridges behaving like printing presses. **Chainflip** halted swaps after a security monitor flagged roughly **$4.86 million** in Ethereum vault outflows, later widening the scope to include **122.86 BTC** and acknowledging a suspected key compromise. FLIP dropped around 30 to 34 percent. Chainflip is offering the attacker a 10 percent bounty to return the rest by Nov. 23 and promising to treat it as a white-hat recovery, which is the polite way of negotiating with someone who already has your money.

The wrapped-asset carousel delivered the week's largest loss. **Wagyu's XMR1** wrapped Monero token collapsed more than 99 percent after an attacker moved 5.2 million tokens out of the deployer account and sold a fraction for roughly **$13.5 million** (Scam Sniffer pegged proceeds near $13.3 million in USDC). The operator, PerpetualCow, blamed a web-host breach and pledged personal refunds.

Two more mint-and-dump stories rounded out the ledger. **Cyber** reported unexpected CYBER minting on Solana followed by tokens leaving its Ethereum bridge lockbox, roughly **$1 million** by The Block's count, prompting **Upbit** and **Bithumb** to freeze transfers. And **Cysic** disclosed a bridge exploit that minted 10 million tokens on **BNB Chain**, with **PeckShield** tracing about **$744,000** to Tornado Cash. Separately, **Fluidkey** published a post-mortem on a **$1.6 million** hack in which stolen session tokens let an attacker stage transfers that users' own apps then signed, with private keys never leaving devices. Bitquery traced at least $1.58 million.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Bitcoin Core node-stalling bug:** Versions **30.5 and 31.3** patch a medium-severity flaw found via Spiral's **Loupe** scanner, where repeating one ID 50,000 times could stall nodes. The retired 29.x branch gets nothing. Notably, an AI tool found it.
- **MONAI Label zero-day:** Suspected China-linked actors used the autonomous pentest framework **Cairn** to discover and exploit an unauthenticated path-traversal flaw in the open-source medical-imaging server (0.8.5 and earlier), gaining root on a university machine. Amazon Threat Intelligence made the attribution with moderate confidence.
- **CVE-2026-99312 (Linux KVM):** Researchers read roughly 127 GB of host memory from inside a guest VM on **DigitalOcean**. Affects AMD hosts with nested virtualization enabled. On shared infrastructure, that is other tenants' memory.
- **GPGME CVE-2026-129975:** A malicious OpenPGP key with an overlong user ID triggers a critical memory bug in the standard GnuPG library, affecting everything using GPGME before 2.3.0.
- **CISA KEV additions:** Four actively exploited flaws across **Apache Roller**, **Cisco Expressway**, **SAP NetWeaver**, and **Broadcom VMware vCenter**, the vCenter deserialization bug at CVSS 9.8.
- **Ghostscript sandbox escape:** pwn.ai demonstrated RCE via crafted file uploads against the interpreter many Linux servers silently use to process PDFs and images.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The headline shift this week is agents doing the attacking, not just the defending. The **MONAI Label** compromise is the cleanest public example yet of autonomous AI tooling (**Cairn**) finding a novel flaw and seizing root, attributed to a suspected China-linked crew. **Bell Canada** separately confirmed an "agentic" threat actor stole enterprise customer data on Nov. 17, including records from its circuit-provisioning system.

On the laundering front, **Elliptic** reports the **Binance** hack proceeds are being moved aggressively, with roughly **$149 million** in bitcoin pushed through coinjoins since Nov. 17, though little appears cashed out. Commodity crime kept pace: the **BADBOX** botnet returned on generic Android TV boxes at a record 40 billion daily ad requests, and **ThreatFabric** detailed **Bazuka**, a full Android malware framework with LLM integration for managing victims at scale. The SEC also flagged a more analog scam: eight Colorado-registered shells, likely run from overseas, faked SEC certificates and filings to defraud investors of over **$37 million**.

## The Bigger Picture

Price did the talking. **Bitcoin** printed a two-year low near **$55,100** on Coinbase and Kraken, roughly 35 percent off its Oct. 21 high, before a bounce back above $60,000 that promptly faded. **CoinGlass** tallied a liquidation wave that ran from $1.8 billion to $2.38 billion depending on the window, with longs accounting for 92.6 percent. US spot bitcoin ETFs bled **$582 million** in a day, led by BlackRock's **IBIT**, and **JPMorgan** notes the drop widens the gap to its estimated $86,000 production cost, squeezing miners. **Strategy's** stash sat roughly $16.8 billion underwater.

The crypto selloff was downstream of the AI trade cracking. **Anthropic's** historic IPO (raising $93.5 billion gross) popped then sank toward its $83 offer price, while **Nvidia** fell to a 52-week low on reports the SEC is probing its accounting. The two stories converge: markets are repricing AI, and crypto caught the draft.

On the quantum watch that everyone insists is years away, **Google's Willow** chip beat the classical ceiling in a coordination game, a hardware test rather than a commercial computer. **CoinDesk** correctly notes the real exposure is not just future key-cracking but bridges, custodians, and the millions of coins in reused addresses, the same plumbing already getting drained the old-fashioned way.

Regulation stayed busy: the US Senate passed the **DEATH BETS Act** curbing prediction-market contracts on war and death, 44 state AGs challenged the CFTC's prediction rules, **Hong Kong** moved to license custodians and advisers, and the **ECB** opened its **Appia** tokenization experiments (applications due Dec. 11). Priced in, all of it, by the time you read the headline.
