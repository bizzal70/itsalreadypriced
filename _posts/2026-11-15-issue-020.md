*Issue #020 · Week of November 15, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The wallet you bought to stay offline shipped with a cellular modem to phone home your seed, and the exploit that poisoned a Claude Code extension started with somebody else's leaked key. The lesson this week is not that the threats are new. It is that they arrive through your suppliers now, while the market was already down 22% on the year and too busy liquidating to notice.

## The Breaches

**Trezor** confirmed the one that should keep you up. Three **Safe 5** devices sold through Southeast Asian reseller **CryptoBilis** contained malicious hardware implants built to exfiltrate recovery phrases over cellular networks. Not phishing, not firmware, actual silicon added to the board. A fix shipped, but the uncomfortable part is the distribution channel, not the patch.

**Kava Lend** lost roughly **$3.8 million** when attackers inflated **BUSD** collateral to borrow **55.2 million KAVA**, then bridged about **49.2 million KAVA** to **BNB Chain** via **LayerZero** and dumped it for roughly $2.1 million in **BNB** and **USDT**. **KAVA** fell as much as 47%, and three Korean exchanges suspended bridge withdrawals and transfers. **Pennysia** users were told to revoke approvals after an exploit **SlowMist** flagged, with swaps already disabled onchain and four addresses drained.

Not technically a breach, but worth watching: **Unocoin** suspended services while probing wallet transfers, and **BitGo** denied any breach, saying the disputed movements used **Unocoin**-held keys. **Unocoin** suspects misuse of a backup mechanism. **Coinone**, meanwhile, warned Korean users of identity theft after a support-side breach exposed passwords, bank account numbers, national ID numbers, and wallet addresses, while insisting its trading and wallet systems were untouched. The data is already out; the phishing follows.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Cursor extension marketplace poisoned via a third party.** A stolen access key, issued to **Fireworks AI** and exposed in a breach there, let an attacker copy data and distribute a trojanized official **Claude Code** extension to as many as 5,000 users. Rotate credentials and check machines if you were in that blast radius.
- **PostgreSQL** patched 52 flaws, including **CVE-2026-6460**, a 22-year-old type-confusion bug (CVSS 8.8) enabling authenticated RCE. Full remediation requires rebuilding C-language extensions, so patching the binary is not the end of it.
- **Apache** shipped a fix for a critical bug that lets unauthenticated attackers run code on HTTP/2-enabled servers, credited to **Anthropic's OSS Scanner** and two Google Cloud researchers. A rare case of an AI scanner earning its keep.
- **UnrealIRCd** flaw **CVE-2026-114713** (CVSS 8.6) landed in **CISA's KEV** catalog under active exploitation. Old protocol, new entry.
- **Windows 'SignDrop'** local privilege escalation remains unpatched; independent testers reproduced it, though exposure depends on Windows build and **CLFS** settings. **Check Point** gateways were separately hit via **PHP2Shell** to deploy a **VMware ESXi** wiper.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The headline takedown: the **US government** disrupted a **North Korean** social engineering operation dubbed **FaceStealer**, tied to more than **$400 million** in crypto theft. In parallel, the **DOJ** moved to seize **$1.2 billion** connected to the **Binance** hack and disrupted a **DPRK** deepfake scheme. Credit where due, but understand the sequencing: the money moved first, the seizure announcement came after, and most of the $1.2 billion will be a long legal road.

The **Binance** heist keeps radiating outward. The same social-engineering-plus-malware playbook that drained **$6.3 billion** on October 29 showed up days later at French lender **Crédit Mutuel Arkéa**, which lost roughly **€100 million**. The supply-chain and social-engineering vectors do not respect the line between crypto and TradFi. Separately, **TRON**, **Tether**, and **Chainalysis** launched a joint Digital Crime Task Force, which is either proactive disruption or a press release, depending on how cynical you are feeling.

## The Bigger Picture

The market already priced the fear. **Bitcoin** fell to a four-month low near **$62K**, down 22% on the year and heading for a sixth straight weekly loss, while crypto shed **$571 billion** in ten days. **US spot Bitcoin ETFs** bled about **$2.1 billion** in November across a seven-day outflow streak, with **BlackRock's IBIT** accounting for roughly $1.42 billion of it and an 11-session losing run. **Ether** slid under $1,900, **XRP** hit its lowest since November 2024, **SOL** touched $67, and **CoinGlass** logged **$2.05 billion** in liquidations, overwhelmingly longs. **Strategy's** cost gap on its stack widened to **$7.7 billion**, though that threshold triggers no margin call by itself.

The macro backdrop is the Fed. **US PPI** printed at 6.5%, and despite cooler-than-forecast headline readings, rate markets still leaned toward a December hike. The **Fed's** own survey now ranks **AI** as the top financial-stability risk, cited by 87% of contacts, up from 50% in spring, and **Nvidia** is leaderless after **Jensen Huang's** abrupt exit amid a still-contested espionage narrative. When the AI trade sneezes, the crypto beta catches it.

On the slower-moving board: **Coinbase** won final **OCC** clearance for a US national trust company, **Deutsche Bank** turned on crypto custody for paying clients, and **Kazakhstan** plans tokenized government bonds after a $22 million note sale. Regulatory relief from Washington is not coming soon; insiders are bracing for investigations in the lame-duck session rather than a **Clarity Act** revival. And **Ethereum** is weighing a **Hegotá** reset for post-quantum security, with development described as "on pause," a reminder that the quantum threat is a slow problem nobody prices until it is suddenly due.
