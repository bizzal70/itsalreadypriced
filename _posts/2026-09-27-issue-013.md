*Issue #013 · Week of September 27, 2026*

## This Week's Verdict

The most expensive lesson this week cost **Bitget** $388 million, and the attacker never touched a private key. When your ledger trusts a forged internal transfer request, cold storage is theater. Everyone froze wallets after the money was gone, which is roughly the point of this newsletter.

## The Breaches

**Bitget** is the headline, and it deserves the space. On September 24 at 18:31 UTC, attackers drained the exchange's hot and warm wallets. The number started at $351.6 million, then crept to $387.5 million once **Bitget** finished counting assets on **Zcash** and **TRON**. CEO **Gracy Chen** confirmed the mechanism: not stolen keys, but spoofed internal transfer requests fed to a compromised backend. **XRP** and **ether** dominated the outflows. Cold wallets were untouched, which is the only sentence in the postmortem that reads like a win. Withdrawals remain paused.

The freeze effort tells the real story. **Circle** and **Tether** blacklisted a wallet tagged "Bitget Exploiter 8" and locked roughly $318,000 in **USDC** and **USDT**. Against $388 million, that is a rounding error, and it happened because the attacker had already swapped the bulk into unfreezable **ETH**. **Ripple** could not freeze the stolen **XRP** either, and the address then completed an **XRP**-to-bitcoin swap through **THORChain** despite a freeze demand from **Bitget** (more on that below).

Elsewhere, **Duelbits** co-founder reported a $7 million hack, with the casino offline and onchain records showing funds consolidated into **ETH**. Balances are reportedly untouched, pending reopening. The recurring theme: everything ends up in **ETH**, because ETH does not have a compliance desk.

## Vulnerabilities Worth Your Attention

- **Magic Eden's legacy approvals** turned into a live exploit. A flaw in **Limit Break's Payment Processor V2**, a contract **Magic Eden** stopped using in October 2024, let an attacker claim any approved NFT for 0 ETH. Nobody watched a dead contract for over 12 hours. Yuga Labs' **0xQuit** whitehatted 23,155 NFTs worth $5.7 million into protective custody. Holders still need to revoke approvals. Your old permissions never expire; your memory of granting them does.
- **KelpDAO's $292M rsETH bridge exploit** is now a lawsuit. **KelpDAO** developer Evercrest is suing **LayerZero** and CEO **Bryan Pellegrino**, alleging **LayerZero** endorsed the single-verifier configuration in writing, then warned a different developer against it. Pellegrino calls it meritless. Either way, "single verifier" and "$292 million" should never share a config file.
- **AlphaFi** is winding down after an oracle error, with new deposits and loans disabled and **Sui Foundation** support arranged. Slush users are told to exit four strategies immediately. Oracle errors remain the quiet killer of DeFi yield products.
- **Cloudflare Containers** cross-tenant flaw let a paying **Workers** customer recover residual disk data left behind by other tenants on the same host. Fixed, no live-workload access, but a reminder that "someone else's leftover disk" is a category that should not exist.

## Threat Actors & Campaigns

The **Bitget** fingerprints point at **DPRK**. Both **Bitget's** CEO and **The Hacker News** name suspected North Korean actors, and the playbook fits: backend compromise, spoofed transfers, rapid consolidation into **ETH**, laundering through cross-chain swaps. **Lazarus** has spent years perfecting the exchange-drain-to-unfreezable-asset pipeline, and this is a textbook run.

On the traditional-crime side, **ShinyHunters** stayed busy, resuming mass exploitation of **Oracle PeopleSoft** (CVE-2026-35273, CVSS 9.8) via a URL-encoding WAF bypass, and separately defacing the **Clop** ransomware leak site through an unpatched **Grav CMS** path traversal. When extortion crews start hacking each other's leak sites, the ecosystem is eating its own.

Also worth noting: **PamStealer** macOS malware added server-side payload decryption, and **Lunex Stealer** abused an **AMD** driver to disable security monitoring while targeting Ukrainian-speaking users through fake CAPTCHA lures. The credential-theft supply chain does not sleep.

## The Bigger Picture

**THORChain** declined **Bitget's** demand to block the hacker's addresses, and the flagged address completed its swap anyway. **OKX's Star Xu** challenged **THORChain's** self-comparison to **Bitcoin**. This is the recurring philosophical fault line: a protocol that cannot censor is also a protocol that launders, and no amount of press-release outrage changes the code. It is the same lesson **Ripple** and the stablecoin issuers just relearned in real time.

On regulation, the **Clarity Act** collapsed in the Senate, and the response was telling. The **SEC**, **CFTC**, and **Fed** moved within days to write the rules themselves. **SEC** staff issued FAQs clarifying that token buybacks and network upgrades do not automatically make a token a security, and that qualifying liquid-staking receipts fall outside security categories. The **Fed** proposed capital charges and bank-approval rules for stablecoin issuers under the **GENIUS Act**, with a two-business-day redemption clock. Meanwhile **Hester Peirce**, the **SEC's** steadiest crypto voice, departs October 2, and the **Blockchain Association** is swapping **Summer Mersinger** for interim CEO **Kristin Smith**. The advocates are leaving just as the rulemaking gets real.

Markets, briefly, because they are context. Spot **Bitcoin** ETFs turned 2026 flows positive with a $2.4 billion weekly haul, their largest since October, erasing a $5.8 billion hole. **BTC** consolidated near $84,000 while altcoins rallied and a $15.6 billion options expiry passed without drama. The tokenization grind continued: **Aave V4** on **Base** added **Coinbase** stock tokens as collateral, and **Ethena** extended **USDe** backing into **Binance** bStocks and equity perpetuals. Wall Street and crypto are now fighting over the same turf, which means the attack surface is about to get a lot more interesting.
