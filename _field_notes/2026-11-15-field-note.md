## Today's Field Note
Kava Lend got opened up through inflated BUSD collateral, letting attackers borrow 55.2 million KAVA (roughly $3.8 million), then bridge about 49.2 million of it to BNB Chain via LayerZero and dump it for BNB and USDT. KAVA dropped close to 40 to 47 percent and three Korean exchanges suspended withdrawals. Separately, South Korean exchange Coinone disclosed a breach exposing passwords, bank account numbers, national ID numbers, and crypto wallet addresses, which is a phishing kit handed to attackers who already know your on-chain footprint. And the US confirmed it disrupted the North Korean FaceStealer social engineering campaign tied to over $400 million in theft, a reminder the recruiter and fake-interview playbook is still running. Collateral oracles, exchange databases, and your DMs are all live attack surfaces today.

## Today's Move
- If you hold or LP on Kava Lend, exit now and revoke any active borrow or collateral approvals on both Kava and BNB Chain.
- Watch the attacker's BNB Chain cash-out path (BNB and USDT from the 49.2M KAVA swap) and avoid buying the KAVA bounce into exchange withdrawal suspensions.
- Coinone users: rotate the password, drop any reused credentials elsewhere, move funds off exposed wallet addresses, and treat every "Coinone support" or recovery message as a phishing attempt.
- Assume leaked national IDs enable SIM swaps. Move off SMS 2FA to hardware or app-based keys today.
- Builders hiring: treat unsolicited recruiters, coding challenges, and "fix my repo" requests as FaceStealer vectors. Run them in a disposable VM, never on a key-bearing machine.
