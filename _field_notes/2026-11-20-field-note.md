## Today's Field Note
OnChain Lens tracked nearly $600 million in ETH moving over two days from the Binance hacker, washed through fresh wallets and cross-chain routers THORChain and Chainflip to break the trail. This is the same playbook Lazarus-adjacent operators run post-Bybit, and it means swap liquidity on those venues is now carrying tainted flow that exchanges will blacklist on arrival. Separately, Wrapped Monero (XMR1) collapsed 99% after an issuer-linked wallet dumped $13.5 million and rotated roughly 7,467 ETH into fresh addresses, which Scam Sniffer flagged as suspected theft rather than ordinary selling. Both are reminders that bridges and newly issued wrapper tokens are where funds quietly leave the building. Nothing here is a protocol exploit, but the laundering corridors and the rug mechanics are the operational signal today.

## Today's Move
- If you ran THORChain or Chainflip swaps in the last 48 hours, check your received funds against known Binance-hacker clusters before depositing to any CEX, or risk a frozen account.
- Avoid providing liquidity to THORChain or Chainflip pools right now while they are actively processing laundered ETH.
- If you hold XMR1 or any wrapped-Monero wrapper, exit or stop interacting with it; treat issuer-linked wallet activity as adversarial.
- Revoke token approvals for XMR1 and any contracts tied to its issuer using Revoke.cash or Scam Sniffer.
- Watch the flagged recipient address that received 5.2M XMR1 and moved ~4,836 ETH, and tag it in your wallet's blocklist.
