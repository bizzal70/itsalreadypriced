## Today's Field Note
Two custody stories worth your attention. Trezor says a login flaw at its email vendor Brevo let attackers blast a phishing email to 347,000 subscribers; 2,500 clicked the malicious link. Trezor is treating every one of those addresses as compromised and reusable, which means the phishing does not stop with one wave. Separately, Blockstream refused a ransom demand for the roughly 600 BTC still held from the Liquid exploit, opting to work with law enforcement and exchanges instead. Liquid has resumed transactions but peg-outs remain disabled, so anyone with funds bridged into Liquid is still stuck.

## Today's Move
- If you own a Trezor, assume your email is on the leaked list. Treat any message referencing your device, seed, or a "security update" as hostile, and never type a seed anywhere but the device itself.
- Verify firmware and any prompts only through Trezor Suite directly, not links in email. Bookmark the real domain and stop trusting inbound mail entirely.
- If you hold assets on Liquid, do not expect peg-outs today. Hold, do not attempt workarounds, and watch Blockstream's official channel for the peg-out re-enable.
- Watch the Liquid exploit address for movement of the 600 BTC; flag any flow into exchange deposit wallets so it can be frozen.
- Same phishing wave hit BitBox and CoinTracking subscribers via Brevo. If you use either, apply the same email-hostile posture and rotate account passwords.
